# Optional seven-category weighted MOT evaluation — 100 points

**Use only when explicitly requested** as a category-by-category breakdown, qualitative cross-check or alternative 100-point assessment. The owner's **fixed-deduction score is the canonical default** for comparisons between cars. Never blend points from this table into the fixed score or silently substitute this more subjective result. Scores here reflect professional judgement on supplied MOT evidence; **they are not official DVSA ratings or independently validated risk estimates**.

Use **complete annual testing cycles**, combining fails and close subsequent passes/retests. Distinguish a historically failed defect from a defect unresolved in the latest recorded evidence. Score based on severity, repeated patterns and corroborated repairs rather than a raw count of failed tests. Give a brief evidence reason next to each category if this breakdown is shown. Avoid false precision: when data are too sparse for a responsible category judgement, mark that category **insufficient evidence**, do not fabricate the full 100-point result; report the fixed method as provisional if applicable.

## Categories

| Category | Weight | Evidence-based ranges and interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| **Structural rust and corrosion** | **20** | **20** no structural corrosion recorded (not proof rust-free); **14–18** isolated minor corrosion advisory; **8–13** multiple corrosion advisories; **3–7** recurring subframe/structural corrosion; **0–3** serious structural corrosion failure. Escalating corrosion over several years is a major red flag. |
| **Mechanical condition and suspension** | **20** | **20** no significant recorded mechanical defects; **16–19** occasional normal wear; **10–15** several worn components; **4–9** recurring steering/suspension/mechanical defects; **0–3** serious unresolved safety-critical mechanical concerns. Repaired isolated coil spring less concerning than persistent steering play. Includes suspension arms, ball joints, bearings, steering, springs and shock absorbers. |
| **Maintenance and neglect indicators** | **20** | **18–20** strong **documented** preventative maintenance; **14–17** generally maintained; **9–13** mixed evidence; **4–8** repeated indications of neglect; **0–3** persistent serious neglect. Inferences from MOT history alone are limited: do not award 18–20 merely because tests were clean. Repeated three-year advisories, illegal tyres, simple repeated lighting failures and multiple unrelated safety defects warn of reactive care. Disappearing advisories do not prove a specific repair without invoices. |
| **MOT failure history** | **15** | **15** no failures in representative available history; **13–14** one minor-component failure; **10–12** occasional easily remedied fails; **5–9** multiple significant mechanical fails; **0–4** repeated serious/dangerous failure cycles. Count **annual cycles**, not a fail and pass as separate years. Severity matters more than quantity. |
| **Tyres and brakes** | **10** | **10** no significant recorded concerns; **8–9** occasional tyre/brake wear; **5–7** recurring wear advisories; **2–4** dangerous tyre or major brake defects; **0–1** repeated dangerous defects. Uneven tyre wear may suggest alignment/suspension trouble, not a diagnosis. Routine tyres alone are not proof of neglect. |
| **Mileage consistency** | **10** | **10** consistent annual progression; **7–9** small explainable discrepancies; **4–6** unexplained irregularities; **0–3** substantial unexplained recorded reduction. Use exact dates, odometer units and retest checks; estimate miles/year when possible; never assert mileage tampering from readings alone. Low mileage is not automatically mechanically better. |
| **Most recent MOT** | **5** | **5** pass with no advisories; **4** pass with limited minor advisories; **2–3** pass with multiple wear advisories; **1** pass with significant outstanding advisories; **0** latest test with serious defects unresolved **in the provided record**. A pass after a fail establishes tested compliance then, but does not prove every advisory was repaired. |

Category maxima total **100**: 20 + 20 + 20 + 15 + 10 + 10 + 5. Calculate `weighted total = sum of seven category points`, and `optional weighted score out of ten = total / 10`. A score from this optional method may use tenths; **do not present it as the fixed-deduction score**. A 75/100 weighted estimate becomes **7.5/10 (optional weighted)**, not 7.5 fixed. Explain differences without forcing them to agree.

## Important judgement controls

- **No recorded corrosion** means no MOT-reported corrosion in the records seen, not absence of structural rust. Limited coverage or unreadable tests lowers confidence.
- Do not equate a passed MOT with complete servicing, engine/gearbox reliability or a fault-free car. Invoices and service documents support maintenance judgments.
- Do not invent a category's condition because data are missing; missing mileage data in particular must not default automatically to 10/10. If fewer than two annual cycles exist, mark any numerical comparison provisional/Low confidence.
- Do not award or subtract duplicate points in the same category for differently worded versions of one defect. Some overlap between categories is intrinsic to the owner's **optional qualitative framework**; describe distinct reasoning and **never** add these losses to the separate fixed method.
- A severe unresolved brake, steering or structural defect must receive an explicit safety warning and an appropriate buying recommendation regardless of an aggregate weighted score. For any published condition score, retain the fixed method's safety caps and make it clear which score is authoritative.
- Compare two cars numerically **using the same method and evidence standard**. Earlier holistic estimates need recalculation before strict comparison.

## Optional display

Only when asked, show a concise seven-row score table with evidence, `total /100`, `optional weighted /10`, a reminder that the fixed-deduction score is the canonical figure, and a short uncertainty note. The standard buyer report stays approximately 120–180 words and uses **only** the fixed score.
