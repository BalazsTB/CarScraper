# Repair estimates and financial viability — UK

Use **broad ranges** in GBP for typical independent UK garages when current vehicle/model/local quotations are unavailable. Parts, labour and VAT may vary significantly. Check credible recent sources if accessible and name the locality or model adjustment only when evidenced. These are *indicative guides*, not price quotes or guaranteed work needed.

| Possible job, only when supported by records | Approximate UK cost |
| --- | --- |
| Simple lighting/bulb fault | £10–£100 |
| Tyres | £70–£180 **per tyre** |
| Brake pads and discs | £180–£450 **per axle** |
| Suspension part (spring, bush, arm, ball joint, shock etc.) | £150–£500 **per component** |
| Complex multi-component suspension work | £800–£1,500 or more |
| Exhaust section | £180–£450 |
| Catalytic converter | £300–£1,100+ **only if diagnosed** |
| Structural corrosion repair/welding | £400–£2,000+, possibly substantially higher |

**Brakes and steering:** Specific cost may not be quantifiable from an MOT note alone, especially pipe corrosion, ABS or rack defects. Seek a written assessment, rather than applying unrelated brake-pad prices. **Emissions:** Do not price a new DPF, catalyst or injector as the expected repair merely because the vehicle failed a smoke or emissions test; diagnosis may be necessary. **Tyres:** Ordinary replacement is routine wear; unusual repeated illegal tyres can be a maintenance warning, not proof of broader neglect.

## Time horizons

1. **Immediate / MOT-required:** Defects still unresolved in the latest available MOT evidence or independently verified current problems. If a current dangerous defect is recorded, do not suggest driving the car until made safe and lawfully compliant.
2. **Next 12 months, potential:** Current advisories and credible recurring concerns with a plausible priced intervention. Mark as *potential*, not certain; time horizon is an estimate.
3. **Longer-term / diagnosis and inspection:** Historic failures that passed on retest, emerging patterns without current symptoms, hidden mechanical conditions. Usually exclude these from the combined numeric range until enough evidence exists.

**Combining the range:** Add the lower/upper estimates **only for independently supported jobs within the same stated horizon**. E.g. if two tyres are clearly indicated and one independently defective exhaust section is current, two tyre jobs £140–£360 plus exhaust £180–£450 ⇒ total £320–£810. Do not sum a hypothetical converter and a hypothetical sensor as simultaneous likely emissions repair. Do not count a spring twice as a generic suspension component and complex suspension work. Keep uncertain diagnostic issues separately identified; an open-ended severe corrosion quote may legitimately use `+` at the upper bound. If no documented action is reasonably supportable, say “£0 identified from MOT defects; underlying repair needs unknown,” not “no repairs needed.”

## Value-based guidance (independent of mechanical score)

Compare supportable repair spending with **realistic UK market value of a comparable roadworthy example**, if known. Distinguish this from the seller's asking price, which may be a poor value proxy.

`repair-to-value % = relevant repair-cost estimate ÷ roadworthy market value × 100`

For a range of costs, a **range of percentages** is normally more honest. The resulting warning can span more than one band; say so. Do not invent a denominator or calculate any ratio when no credible roadworthy value is available.

| Repair cost ÷ roadworthy vehicle value | Practical interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Below 50% | Potentially economical, depending on future reliability |
| 50–69% | Financial caution |
| 70–99% | Strong warning about repair economics |
| 100% or more | Repairs may be uneconomical |

These are **guidelines, not absolute rules**; consider scarcity/collectability, actual roadworthy replacement costs, potential additional work, concurrent repairs and what an independent inspection reveals. Do not extrapolate a high repair ratio into a claim that the vehicle must be scrapped.
